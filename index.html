01 / The game

To mark the company's 30th anniversary, we built a game on its website. Thirty hidden items, each a symbol of a famous TV show, were placed on the show pages.

Every day a new item went live with a riddle-style hint. The player found the item on the site, clicked it, and it was added to their personal collection in their user account. Players who collected all 30 items became eligible for prizes. The main winner was determined by the timestamp of the 30th find: the player who found the final item first won.

The project included a landing page, a new "Game" section in the user account, daily hints across four channels (social media, email, account, landing page), a leaderboard and sharing of progress.

05 / Workflow and edge cases

What the player sees in every situation

I specified the behaviour for every situation I could find, including the ones that rarely happen.

When a player clicks a hidden item
SituationWhat the user sees
Not logged inPopup: "Congratulations! You found an item from the game. Collect the whole collection and win a prize! Log in or create an account to save the item to your profile", with a "Log in" button. After registration the item is added automatically
Logged in, first findPopup: "Congratulations! The item has been added to your collection", with a "View" button. The time of the find is recorded, which is how winners are determined
Item already found (for example, the game was opened in another browser)Popup: "Item already found", with a "View" button
What the account page shows
SituationMessage
Found everything available so far"You've completed all available tasks, well done! Come back tomorrow!"
Found all 30 items"You found all 30 items! Congratulations!" plus the announcement date for winners
Found some or noneA task for one of the missing items, under the heading "Try to find this item!"

User workflow

  1. 00:00 Moscow time: a new task and hint go live on the landing page, in the account, on social media and by email.
  2. The player reads the riddle and guesses which show it describes.
  3. They go to that show's page, find the hidden item and click it.
  4. The system checks who clicked: a logged-out visitor, a logged-in player with a new item, or a player who already has it (see the tables).
  5. The account page shows the collection: found items marked, the count out of 30 and the time of each find. The player can share the result as a card.
  6. After the 30th item the player becomes a contender. The winner is whoever found the 30th item first.

06 / Working with the team

Checking the result and agreeing scope

Bug report after release

I checked the live landing page on mobile and found the tasks gallery could not be swiped. I reported it to the developer with a screenshot and pointed to the requirement and the design: the user must see all tasks, and the designer's file included navigation arrows.

Adapting to the developer

A reviewer's comments arrived scattered across the document. The developer preferred one separate list, so I consolidated them before the handoff.

Scope under time pressure

After the game I needed to announce the winners on the landing page. A new design block was not realistic in the time left, so I proposed reusing the existing banner button with new text pointing to the results news. The developer confirmed it was feasible.

07 / Sample task

A riddle, not a name

Were the tasks clear? By day 7, with the number of players growing, we concluded they were clear because no one contacted support about them. I had defined the KPIs in advance (players with at least one item, all participants, players who found all 30, the most popular item).

09 / What I learned

Four lessons from the project

Promotion, not the daily mechanic, drove growth. Player numbers jumped on days with on-air or messaging promotion and stayed flat otherwise. Next time I would plan the promo calendar together with the task calendar, so the strongest items and the biggest promo moments coincide.

Edge cases belong in the first draft. The "item already found" scenario surfaced during review, and publication time across Russia's 11 time zones only after release, when I added the midnight Moscow time rule. Time-dependent behaviour needs to be specified up front.

Legal review shapes the interface. Rule acknowledgement changed a main button and the landing copy. I would involve legal and finance before writing the first version of the copy, not after.

Search was the largest source of visits (47%). My hypothesis is that players searched for hints, which would mean riddle quality directly affected reach.

10 / What I would improve

Next time

  • Make the game shorter, for example 7 days.
  • Launch in prime time, in the evening when users are active.
  • Add communication channels: email, Telegram, push notifications and a feedback form.
  • Strengthen marketing:
    • promo in the account and in popups;
    • warm-up through mailings and social media;
    • promotion through QR codes and partner platforms;
    • collaboration with the social networks VK or OK.
  • Add an in-interface reminder: "Share on VK/OK and get a bonus" (for example, a hint, an extra chance or an easier task).
  • Involve media personalities.
  • Add intermediate bonuses to keep players interested.

Scaling potential

  • The collecting mechanic is already implemented technically.
  • The game adapts easily to other events.
  • It suits retail activations, quests and online marathons.
